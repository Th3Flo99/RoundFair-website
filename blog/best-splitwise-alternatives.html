Best Splitwise Alternatives (and When to Switch)

2026-09-19 · By FairLead

The best Splitwise alternative depends on why you are leaving. Choose Tricount, Settle Up or Splid for trips and ledgers, a payment app for simple transfers, or RoundFair if what you really need is a fair rotation for buying rounds.

Splitwise is popular for good reason, but it is one answer to one problem: keeping a running ledger of who owes whom. If your problem is different, or if your group will not sign up, a different tool will serve you better. This guide covers why people look elsewhere, which alternatives fit which job, whether you need a ledger at all, and how to switch without losing track of what is owed.

Why people look for an alternative

  • Free tier limits or prompts to upgrade
  • Too much setup for a single evening out
  • Friends who refuse to create an account
  • Wanting less tracking and more privacy
  • A different job: rounds, not a ledger

Notice that only the first item is really about Splitwise itself. The others are about a mismatch between the tool and the occasion. A ledger is built for weeks of shared spending, and asking it to handle one Friday at the pub is like using a spreadsheet to write a text message.

If you want another ledger

Tricount, Settle Up and Splid are the ledger-style alternatives most often mentioned. They follow the same idea as Splitwise: enter each expense, say who shares it, and let the app work out the settlement. Compare them on the points that actually differ between apps, which are free-tier limits, whether an account is required, how many currencies are supported, and whether friends can join without installing anything.

Spend a few minutes creating a test group with real numbers from your last trip. The app that makes that easiest is the one your friends will actually use.

If you only need to move money

Many people use a ledger when all they wanted was to get paid back. A payment app does that directly: work out the shares, send a request with a clear note, done. There is no group to create and no history to maintain. Our guide to splitting a bill and charging friends on Venmo shows the steps, and how to remind friends to pay you back handles the friendly nudge.

Do you need a ledger at all?

A ledger is right when many people pay for many things over days or weeks. If your group simply takes turns buying, a ledger creates work without adding fairness. Turn-taking evens out by itself. Our guide to round buying vs splitting the bill explains when each wins.

RoundFair is designed for that second case. It shows whose turn it is with its Up now view, offers a spin wheel to pick a buyer at random, and splits a bill when someone wants to settle. It is free on iOS, needs no account, and stores data on your device. It is not a replacement for a long-running household ledger, and does not try to be. Switching without losing the plot

  1. Settle all open balances in your old app first.
  2. Export or screenshot the history if you want a record.
  3. Start the new group fresh with the same members.
  4. Tell everyone at once, so expenses are not split across two apps.
  5. Keep the old app installed for a month in case a late expense turns up.

The biggest risk when switching is a half-settled balance living in two places. Clear the old ledger completely, even if it means everyone sends a small payment today, and the new one starts clean.

What to look for in any replacement

  • Speed. Can you add an expense in a few taps?
  • Friction for friends. Do they need an account?
  • Clarity. Does the final settlement make sense at a glance?
  • Cost. What does the free version really allow?
  • Privacy. Where is your data stored, and who can see it?

Run your last real trip or night out through the candidate app before you commit. Five minutes of realistic testing reveals more than any feature list.

A closer look at the alternatives

The alternatives fall into a few groups, and understanding the groups is more useful than memorising app names, since features change often. Check each app's own page for the current details.

Other ledger apps

These follow the same model as Splitwise: groups, expenses, balances and a suggested settlement. Differences show up in free-tier limits, currency support, whether an account is required and how easy it is to invite friends. They are the closest substitutes if you like the ledger idea and want a different set of trade-offs.

Payment apps

Apps such as Venmo, PayPal, Revolut and Tikkie do not track fairness, but they are excellent at the last step: asking for and sending money. If your real pain is chasing people for payment, this is the tool to reach for.

Spreadsheets and notes

The most flexible and the least convenient. They suit small groups with a patient organiser and a single trip.

Round trackers

A specialised category for groups that take turns. RoundFair sits here, and does one thing well: it shows whose turn it is and splits the bill when needed.

Signs you have outgrown a ledger

  • Friends stop logging expenses, and the balances are out of date.
  • Every new group requires everyone to install and sign up.
  • You mostly use it for nights out, where the ledger feels like overkill.
  • You are paying for a premium tier for features you rarely use.
  • The group has drifted from "trip" to "just Fridays".

If two or more of these ring true, a lighter tool may serve you better. It does not mean the ledger was wrong; it may simply have been right for a different stage of the group.

How to choose in ten minutes

  1. Write down the last three times you split money with friends.
  2. Label each: trip, household, dinner or rounds.
  3. Count how many of each. The most common type decides your main tool.
  4. Pick one app for that type and test it with a real example.
  5. Keep a payment app for the actual transfers.

This exercise usually reveals that most groups have one dominant pattern, and that the tool for it is simpler than the one they were using. If most of your entries are nights out, you probably want a round tracker. If most are flat bills, stay with a ledger.

Privacy considerations when switching

A ledger holds a detailed history of who you spend time with and what you spend. When you leave an app, consider what happens to that history. Export or screenshot what you need, then check whether the app offers a way to delete your data. Prefer new tools that keep data on your device, need no account and explain clearly what they store. RoundFair stores data on your device and in your personal iCloud, has no servers of its own and has no access to your sessions, rounds or balances.

Questions to ask about any Splitwise alternative

  • Is it designed for my situation, or is it a general tool I am bending?
  • Can it handle a friend who does not install it?
  • How does it deal with uneven splits and different currencies, if I need them?
  • What is the free tier's limit, and how likely am I to hit it?
  • Does it work offline?
  • Where does my data live?

Write down the answers for two or three candidates, and the right choice is usually obvious.

Alternatives for nights out specifically

Most Splitwise alternatives are also ledgers, so if the reason you are leaving is that a ledger is too heavy for nights out, another ledger will not help. The alternative for that case is a different kind of tool: a round tracker. It follows whose turn it is rather than logging every purchase, so it stays out of the way. RoundFair is one such tool, free on iOS and requiring no account. Its Up now view shows whose turn it is and who is behind, its spin wheel picks a buyer at random, and Split the bill settles the night at the end.

What you may lose by switching

Be honest about the trade-offs. A lighter tool loses long-term history: you will not have a record of who paid for what over months. It may lose web access, multi-currency support or receipt features. Consider whether you actually use those things. If you do, keep the ledger for those jobs and add a lighter tool for the rest. Switching is not all or nothing. Many people end up with two tools, each for the job it does best, and that is a perfectly sensible outcome.

A short glossary

  • Ledger: a running record of expenses and balances.
  • Settle up: pay what you owe so balances return to zero.
  • Simplified debts: a set of payments that clears everyone with the fewest transfers.
  • Round tracker: a tool that records whose turn it is to buy.
  • Payment request: a message asking a friend to send a specific amount.

Knowing these terms makes it easier to compare apps and to explain to friends what you are using.

Alternatives when your group is not on the same phone platform

A common obstacle is that friends use different phones. A tool that only runs on one platform can leave part of the group out. Before you switch, check which platforms each candidate supports. Ledger apps often cover several. RoundFair is iOS only, so it suits groups where the person keeping the record has an iPhone, and other people can simply look at the screen. If the group is mixed and everyone needs their own access, choose a cross-platform ledger for shared costs, and use RoundFair on one phone for the pub. Being pragmatic about platforms avoids the frustration of a tool that half the group cannot use.

What does it really cost to switch away from Splitwise?

The price of an app is only part of it. The bigger cost is coordination. Suppose your group of six has a shared house ledger with three months of history and a balance where two people owe €38 and €12 to a third. Moving apps means someone has to settle that, tell everyone, and convince five people to install something new. That is easily an evening of nagging spread over a week.

Now compare a group that only uses a ledger for holidays. Their balances are settled after each trip, so switching between trips costs nothing. If that is you, the best time to change apps is right after you settle up, not in the middle of a trip.

A useful rule: switch when the pain of the current app is bigger than the coordination cost, and never mid-trip. If your only complaint is that your friends will not sign up, changing to another app with an account requirement fixes nothing. Choose one that does not need accounts, or nominate one person to enter everything.

What mistakes should I avoid when choosing an alternative?

  • Comparing feature lists instead of testing. Enter one real bill and see how it feels.
  • Assuming a limit is permanent. Free tiers and prices change. Verify on the app's own page.
  • Ignoring what your friends will tolerate. The best app is the one the least enthusiastic person will actually open.
  • Running two apps at once. Expenses split across two places leave nobody sure what is owed.
  • Forgetting the payment step. A ledger tells you who owes what; you still need a way to send the money. See how to split and charge friends on Venmo.
