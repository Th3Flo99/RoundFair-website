RoundFair vs Splitwise: Which Should You Use?

2026-09-19 · By Florian Ronsse

Splitwise is a ledger: it records every expense and calculates who owes whom. RoundFair is a round tracker: it shows whose turn it is to buy and splits a bill when needed. Use Splitwise for households and trips, RoundFair for nights out.

These two apps are often compared, but they are not really competitors. They answer different questions and shine in different situations. This honest comparison explains the core difference, when each one is the better choice, how the setup and the account requirements differ, and how many groups use both without any overlap.

The core difference

The two apps solve different problems. Splitwise answers who owes whom, in total. RoundFair answers whose turn is it now.

Two philosophies of fairness

A ledger achieves fairness by recording everything. Every expense is entered, every share is calculated, and the app tells you the simplest way to settle. It is precise, and it needs everyone to keep entering data.

A round tracker achieves fairness by rotation. Each person buys a round in turn, and over an evening it evens out. RoundFair adds a light layer on top: it shows whose turn it is with its Up now view, offers a spin wheel to pick a buyer at random, and can split the bill when the rounds have been uneven. The trade-off is that it is built for an evening, not for months of household costs. For the deeper comparison of approaches, see round buying vs splitting the bill.

When Splitwise wins

  • A week-long trip with many shared costs
  • Rent, bills and groceries with flatmates
  • Groups that need history over months
  • Multiple payers and many small expenses that must be tallied precisely

If your problem is "we have spent money on different things over several weeks and need to know who owes what", a ledger is the right tool, and Splitwise or one of its alternatives will serve you well. See best Splitwise alternatives.

When RoundFair wins

  • A Friday at the pub where everyone takes a turn
  • Deciding fairly who buys next, see whose turn to buy the next round
  • Nights where some do not drink, see splitting when some friends don't drink
  • Groups where friends will not create an account

RoundFair is free on iOS, needs no account, and keeps your data on your device. It works with one phone on the table and with no signal, which suits the sort of bar where a ledger would not even load.

Setup and friction

The difference shows up in the first minute. A ledger asks you to create a group, add members and then enter expenses. That is worth it for a trip and a burden for a pub. RoundFair asks you to add friends and start a round. There is no group to configure and no account to create, so the app is usable before the first drink arrives.

Friction matters because groups abandon tools that slow the evening down. The best app is the one your friends will actually let you use.

Can you use both?

Yes. Many groups keep a ledger for the holiday and use RoundFair for the evenings out. They do not overlap much. The ledger holds the flights, the rental and the groceries; RoundFair handles who buys the next round and how the bar bill splits. If you want more options, see the best bill splitting apps and free bill splitting apps.

Which should you install first?

If you go out with friends more than you share a household, start with RoundFair: it is free, needs no account and is useful on the first night. If you live with other people or plan a long trip, a ledger will earn its keep faster. Neither excludes the other, and neither costs much to try. The honest test is simple: after a month, which app did your group actually open? Use that one, and add the other only when a specific situation calls for it.

What each app is like on a typical night

With a ledger: the group creates or opens a shared group, and someone enters each expense as it happens, choosing who paid and who shares it. At the end, the app shows the balances and a suggested set of payments. It is accurate and thorough, and it depends on people doing the data entry.

With RoundFair: you add friends, start a session and log each round as it is bought. The Up now view shows whose turn it is and who is behind. If nobody wants to decide, the spin wheel picks. At the end, Split the bill works out who owes whom, with a consumption overview showing who had what and the cost per person. It is quick during the night and light at the end.

Neither is wrong. They reflect different priorities: completeness for one, ease for the other.

Cost, accounts and data

Check each app's own page for current pricing and limits, since they change. In general, ledger apps offer a free tier with optional paid features, and often expect an account. RoundFair is free on iOS with no account required. For data, RoundFair stores everything on your device and in your personal iCloud, has no servers of its own and no access to your sessions, rounds or balances. Sharing a session uses Apple's iCloud (CloudKit), so the group's data stays inside Apple's ecosystem. If you care about a minimal footprint, that is a real difference. If you need web access or a large ecosystem, a ledger app may suit you better.

How to decide in a group chat

If your group is debating which app to use, ask one question: "What will we mostly use it for?" If the answer is a trip or a shared home, a ledger. If it is nights out, a round tracker. If it is both, use both. The discussion is usually settled in a minute, and everyone feels heard.

To avoid a fight, do not turn it into a contest. Nobody is losing if you pick a different tool for a different job. The aim is a system that gets used, so let the group choose what they are most likely to open. For related advice, read the best bill splitting apps.

Frequently mixed-up ideas

  • "A ledger is always more accurate." Only if everyone logs everything. A simple tool used consistently beats a thorough tool used rarely.
  • "Rounds are always fair." Only with similar drinks and a similar pace. Otherwise track amounts.
  • "Apps replace etiquette." They do not. They just make fairness easier to see.
  • "You must pick one." You do not. Mix tools as your evenings change.

An honest look at the limits of each

Splitwise's limits: it needs consistent data entry, which is a burden on a pub night. It is designed for groups, not for a quick round at the bar. Free tiers can be limited, so check its current pricing. It typically expects an account, which some friends resist.

RoundFair's limits: it is iOS only. It is not a long-running ledger, does not scan receipts and does not convert currencies. It is built for rounds and bar bills, and it stays in that lane.

Recognising the limits is what allows a fair comparison. A tool that does one job well is worth more than one that does ten badly, and most groups are better served by matching the tool to the job than by expecting one tool to do everything.

Who each app suits, by personality

  • The organiser who loves detail: a ledger will delight them.
  • The friend who hates admin: a round tracker asks for very little.
  • The group with mixed devices: check availability first, since RoundFair is iOS only and ledger apps often cover more platforms.
  • The privacy-minded: favour apps that need no account and keep data on the device.
  • The frequent traveller: a ledger with currency support for trips, plus a round tracker for evenings.

How the two work together on a trip

On a week away, a ledger holds the shared costs: the rental, the groceries, the activities. Each evening at a bar or restaurant, you could open RoundFair to run the rounds. At the end of the trip, the ledger settles the shared costs, and any uneven rounds have already been settled in the evenings. The two tools stay out of each other's way, and each does the job it is best at. It is a realistic setup for a group that wants to stay fair without turning the holiday into an accounting exercise.

Ten questions to decide between them

  1. Are we mostly going on trips or living together? Choose a ledger.
  2. Are we mostly going to the pub? Choose a round tracker.
  3. Do we need history over months? A ledger.
  4. Will friends refuse to sign up? A tool with no account.
  5. Does everyone have an iPhone? RoundFair is iOS only, so check.
  6. Do we need multiple currencies? A ledger with currency support.
  7. Do we need to scan receipts? Check the app's own page; RoundFair does not.
  8. Do we care about privacy and data on the device? Check where each app stores data.
  9. Do we want it fast at the table? A round tracker.
  10. Could we use both? Probably yes.

If most answers point the same way, you have your app. If they split, use both.

A realistic month of using both

Imagine a group of friends over a month. Week one: a night at the pub, tracked with RoundFair, settled at closing time with a single transfer. Week two: a shared dinner, split in a calculator and paid by request. Week three: a weekend away, with expenses in a ledger. Week four: another pub night, tracked in RoundFair again. At the end of the month, the ledger has settled the weekend, the pub nights have settled themselves and the dinner is long forgotten. Nobody has spent an evening arguing about money, and nobody has kept a spreadsheet they hated. That is what the right tools for the right jobs feel like.

A word on trying before you decide

The best way to compare two apps is to use both for a week. Use the ledger for a trip or a shared cost, and use RoundFair for a night out. Notice which one your friends engage with, which one you open without prompting and which one leaves you feeling relaxed at the end. The answers usually make the choice obvious, and they do so with your real group, not an imaginary one. Both are free to try, so the only cost is a little curiosity. And since they do different jobs, you may well end up keeping both.

How would each app handle the same pub night?

Take five friends at a pub over four hours. Six rounds are bought. Ana buys two, Ben, Cleo and Dan buy one each, and Eli buys one. The total is €120.

With a ledger: someone creates a group, adds all five people, then logs six expenses with the payer and the split for each. At the end the app calculates the balances and suggests transfers. It is accurate, but it asks for six entries, at a time when people are busy talking. If two people skipped a round of drinks, someone also has to adjust the split for that round.

With RoundFair: you add the friends and start a round. The app shows whose turn it is, and the spin wheel can pick a buyer at random when nobody is sure. When the night ends, the consumption overview shows who had what, and Split the bill works out the difference if it was uneven. Nothing is entered after the fact, because the record was built as the night went on. The details are on the who drank what page.

The difference is timing. A ledger is best when the entries are made calmly, after the event. A round tracker is best when the entries need to be made in the moment, at the bar, without breaking the flow of the evening.

How would each app handle a week-long trip?

Now reverse it. Six friends share a holiday home for a week. Flights, the rental, a car, groceries, museum tickets and a few dinners, paid by different people over seven days, with one friend joining late. This is where a ledger shines. It records each expense as it happens, handles who shared what, and reduces a hundred payments to a few final transfers at the end. RoundFair does not do this, and it does not pretend to. It does not keep a long-running record of debts or convert currencies, so do not force it into a job it was not built for.

The good news is that the two fit together. The ledger holds the shared trip costs, and RoundFair handles the evenings out. If one dinner turns into a long bar crawl, tracking every drink in the ledger would be exhausting, and tracking it with rounds is easy. See RoundFair for group trips and splitting group trip costs without falling out. For the currency side of trips, read how to split expenses in a foreign currency.

What are the privacy and account differences?

RoundFair needs no account and keeps your data on your device, which means nothing to sign up for, no email address to give and nobody else to invite to a service. Ledger apps generally rely on accounts, since the data is shared across members and stored online so every member sees the same balance. That is a reasonable design for a shared household. It is a heavier ask for a group of people who just want a drink. Check each app's own privacy policy and pricing pages for current details, since these change often.

It also affects how quickly a group adopts a tool. Getting five friends to sign up to anything on a Friday night is difficult. With RoundFair there is no account to create, so nobody has to sign up before the first round. To keep everyone up to date, the live sharing feature shares a session through iCloud.

How do I decide in two minutes?

Ask three questions about your group.

  1. How long does the shared spending last? An evening points to RoundFair. Weeks or months point to a ledger.
  2. How many payers are there? One or two per evening is fine for rounds. Many payers over many days needs a ledger.
  3. Will everyone create an account? If not, a tool without one is more realistic.

If two answers point one way, take that app. If they split, use both: the two do not clash. And if you are still weighing other ledgers, see best Splitwise alternatives and free bill splitting apps before you decide, and always check current features and pricing on each app's own page.

What do the two apps cost?

RoundFair is free on iOS with no account required. Splitwise has offered a free tier alongside a paid plan in the past, but plans and limits change, so check its own page for what is included today rather than relying on a comparison written at a different time. The more useful question is not price but fit: a free tool that does not match your situation costs you time, and a paid tool that does may be worth it.

What RoundFair does not do

To be fair to Splitwise and other ledgers: RoundFair does not maintain a long-running ledger of debts, does not scan receipts and does not convert currencies. If you need those, use a ledger. RoundFair concentrates on the part of the social calendar that ledgers handle badly: buying rounds.

Frequently asked questions

Is RoundFair better than Splitwise?

Neither is better overall. They fit different jobs: ledger vs rounds.

Does RoundFair track debts?

It focuses on turns and splitting a bill, not a long-running ledger of debts.

Is RoundFair free?

Yes, it is free on iOS with no account required.

Can I use RoundFair and Splitwise together?

Yes. Use a ledger for trips and households, and RoundFair for nights out. They do not overlap much.

Which is better for a group trip?

A ledger, since a trip has many payers and expenses. Use RoundFair for the evenings out during the trip.

A round tracker, because entries are made in the moment. A ledger suits longer spending with many payers.

No. RoundFair has no accounts of its own; sharing uses iCloud and your data stays on your device.
