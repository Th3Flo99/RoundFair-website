How to Remind Friends to Pay You Back

2026-09-19 · By Florian Ronsse

Ask early, be specific and keep it light. Send one clear message with the amount and reason, follow up once after a few days, and make paying as easy as possible with a payment link.

Chasing a friend for money is one of the most uncomfortable small tasks in social life. Most people are not avoiding payment; they forgot, or they were waiting for the details. This guide gives you the rules that work, message templates you can copy, advice on handling the repeat forgetter, and ways to avoid the problem in the first place, including on nights out where rounds are involved.

Why people forget to pay

Understanding the reason makes the message easier to write. Almost nobody skips a €15 debt on purpose. The usual causes are: they did not know the exact amount, they meant to pay later and lost track, the request arrived when they were busy, or paying required effort such as finding bank details. Every one of those is fixable by making the request clear, prompt and easy. None of them requires treating a friend as a debtor.

The rules

  1. Ask within a day or two. The longer you wait, the more awkward it gets, and the more likely the friend has forgotten what it was for.
  2. Include the amount and reason. No guessing. "€28 for Saturday's dinner" is easy to act on.
  3. Make it easy. Attach a payment request or link, so paying takes ten seconds.
  4. Follow up once. A second message after three to five days is fair. More than that becomes pressure.
  5. Keep the tone light. You are reminding, not accusing.

Message templates

The first message

"Hey! Dinner on Saturday came to €28 each. Sending you a request now. Thanks!"

The gentle follow-up

"Quick nudge on the €28 from Saturday, no rush if it slipped your mind."

The light-hearted one

"Friendly reminder from your bank of friends: €28, please."

For the repeat forgetter

"I have a hard time keeping track of this stuff. Could you settle the €28 this week?"

For a group

"Thanks everyone for Saturday! Total was €168, so €28 each. Send whenever suits, ideally this week."

Copy, adjust the tone to the friend, and send. A message that sounds like you is better than one that sounds polished.

Choose the right channel

  • A payment app request is the easiest, since it puts the amount and a pay button in one place. See how to split a bill and charge friends on Venmo.
  • A direct message is best for a first nudge with a close friend.
  • A group chat works for shared bills, but avoid naming individuals who have not paid.
  • In person is fine if you will see them soon, but follow up in writing so there is a record.

If they still do not pay

Ask once more, directly and kindly, after a week or so. For small sums, decide honestly whether the friendship matters more than the money. If the amount is significant, be straightforward: "I need this back by Friday, can you send it?" Clear beats hinting. If someone is short of cash, offer a payment plan rather than letting it hang.

Repeated non-payment is information about how you split things with that person in future. Agree to pay on the spot next time, or stop fronting money for them.

A tone guide for different friends

  • Close friends: humour works. A joke about your bank of friends signals that it is not a big deal.
  • Acquaintances or colleagues: stay neutral and specific. Amount, reason, link.
  • Someone short of money: offer flexibility. "No rush, whenever suits this month" keeps the door open without dropping the request.
  • A group where several owe: send one message to everyone with the total and per-person amount, rather than singling out the slowest.

The right tone is the one that keeps the friendship intact and the money moving. If you are unsure, plain and friendly beats clever.

The psychology of asking for money

Most people find asking for money more uncomfortable than owing it. That discomfort makes us delay, and delay makes it worse. A useful reframe is to treat the request as a service: you are giving your friend a clear, easy way to settle something they would like to settle. Seen that way, a prompt request is kindness, not pressure.

It also helps to remember that the person who owes you is probably a little embarrassed too. A light, matter-of-fact message lets them off the hook, whereas silence makes both of you uncomfortable. The friendship survives a clear request far better than a lingering grudge.

Handling different situations

A large sum

Be direct, agree a date and put it in writing. A repayment plan is fine, as long as it is agreed and honoured.

A repeated pattern

If the same friend regularly forgets, ask them to pay on the spot from now on, or stop fronting money for them. Say it kindly and factually.

A group debt

Send one message to all with the amounts, and avoid singling anyone out. Peer pressure works quietly when everyone sees who has paid.

A colleague

Stay professional: amount, reason, payment details, and a deadline that gives them time.

Someone struggling financially

Offer flexibility, and decide whether you want the money back at all. Sometimes the kind answer is to let it go.

Templates for different channels

Text message: "Hi! Just a note that Saturday's dinner was €28 each. I've sent a request. Thanks!"

Group chat: "Thanks all for last night! Total was €168, so €28 each. Details in the request I've just sent."

Email to a colleague: "Hi Sam, a quick note on the team lunch: your share was €14. Payment details below. Thanks!"

Voice or in person: "Oh, before I forget, I sent you a request for Saturday, €28. No rush."

Adapt the tone to the relationship. If it sounds like you, it will be read as friendly. If it sounds like a debt collector, it will be read as hostile.

After the money arrives

Say thank you. A short message closes the loop and makes the next request easier. If you were paid late, do not comment; the point is that it arrived. If you were paid the wrong amount, correct it kindly. And note what worked: if a payment link got paid within an hour and a plain message took a week, use the link next time. Good habits in this small area make every later split smoother.

When you are the one who owes

Being a good payer is the simplest way to avoid ever needing a reminder. Pay quickly, ideally the same day. If you cannot, say so and give a date. If you are not sure of the amount, ask, so the request is clear. And say thank you. A friend who receives a prompt payment and a short thank-you will happily front money for you again.

If you owe money and you have forgotten, apologise briefly and pay at once. Do not make it a big story. "So sorry, sent now" is all it takes. If you cannot afford it, say so honestly and offer a plan. Most people prefer an honest delay to a silent one.

How to make paying frictionless

Much of the delay in paying is friction. Give your friends every advantage. Send a request in a payment app rather than sharing bank details in a message. Include the amount, the reason and a deadline. Use the app your friends already have. If some use different apps, offer a bank transfer as a fallback. Round the amount so nobody needs to hunt for change. The easier it is, the faster it happens. Most late payments are not about reluctance but about a task that was slightly harder than it needed to be.

Money and different friendships

People have very different attitudes to money. Some are meticulous about pennies; others are relaxed. Neither is wrong, but the mismatch is the source of many awkward moments. When you ask a relaxed friend for a small sum, they may be surprised you noticed. When you are relaxed with a meticulous friend, they may quietly keep count. Knowing this helps you judge tone.

If in doubt, be explicit and kind. Say what you would like, and offer an easy way to do it. Explicit requests are rarely offensive, while silent expectations often are. And when you are on the receiving end, remember that the person asking is probably as uncomfortable as you are.

Rounds: the reminder-free way to share costs

The easiest way to avoid reminders is to avoid debts. On nights out, that means taking turns. Rounds are self-settling: each person pays in the moment, and the totals even out over the evening. Build a system so you never have to chase

The best reminder is the one you never send. Build habits that make chasing rare. Request payment the same day, using a payment app. Say the deadline when you make the request. Note who has paid, so you know whom to nudge. Take turns instead of lending money, wherever you can. And be a good example by paying others promptly. Over time, your group will pick up the norm, and requests will be answered quickly and cheerfully. It is much easier to maintain a culture of prompt payment than to fix a culture of delay.

What not to do

  • Do not post about it publicly or in a group chat naming individuals.
  • Do not send repeated messages within a day.
  • Do not bring up old debts in a moment of anger.
  • Do not add interest or penalties among friends.
  • Do not let it fester for months.

Each of these turns a small issue into a bigger one. Stay calm, stay specific and stay kind, and the vast majority of debts are settled without any drama.

Why a friendly tone works

People respond to a request more readily when it feels warm than when it feels formal. A friendly tone tells the friend that the friendship matters more than the money, which makes them want to pay promptly. A stern tone implies distrust, and can provoke defensiveness. That does not mean you need to be flippant. Be clear about the amount, be kind in the phrasing and be firm about the timing. "Thanks for last night, it was great! Sending the request for the €28 now" combines warmth and clarity. Practise it, and you will find asking for money becomes easy, and even a little bit pleasant, because it shows you trust your friends.

What should I say when a friend has ignored my first message?

Silence is usually not defiance. The message was read while they were on a train, meant to be answered and buried under fifty others. So treat the second message as a resend, not a complaint. Restate everything so the friend does not have to scroll: the amount, the reason and how to pay. A good follow-up looks like this: "Hi! Just resending in case it got buried: €28 for Saturday's dinner at Luigi's. Here's the request again. Thanks!"

Notice what it leaves out: no "as I said before", no "still waiting" and no sarcasm. Each of those turns a reminder into a reprimand, and people who feel scolded pay more slowly, not faster. If a week passes and the second message also gets silence, switch channel. A phone call or a comment in person often works where a text has not, because it is harder to miss and easy to answer with "oh, sorry, sending it now".

How do I remind someone about a large amount, not just a round?

Bigger sums need a different approach. A €15 dinner share is a nudge. A €400 share of a holiday rental is a conversation. For larger amounts, do the following.

  1. Say it out loud, early. Agree the amount and the date before the money leaves your account, ideally in writing: "I'll book the flat, your share is €400, could you send it by the 15th?"
  2. Put it in one place. A short message with the total, the number of people, the per-person share and the deadline is easy to find again later.
  3. Offer a plan. If the person cannot pay in full, suggest two or three instalments with dates. It turns a stressful sum into a manageable one.
  4. Keep a record. Save the receipt and the messages. You are unlikely to need them, but having them makes the conversation calm rather than heated.

For trips, a ledger is the sensible tool, and our guide to splitting group trip costs without falling out explains how to set the rules up front.

How many reminders are too many?

A useful rule is one message, one follow-up and one direct conversation. That is three touches, spread over two or three weeks. After that, more messages rarely help and often damage the friendship. Here is a simple timeline for a €28 share.

WhenWhat to do
Same evening or next daySend the request with amount and reason
Day 4 or 5One light nudge that repeats the details
Day 10 to 14Ask directly, in person or by phone if possible
After thatDecide: forgive it, or change how you split with this person

If you find yourself at the last line, remember that the aim is a healthy friendship with sensible money habits, not winning. Forgiving a small sum is a legitimate choice; so is choosing to pay on the spot from then on.

How do I remind friends without sounding passive-aggressive?

Tone is mostly word choice. A few swaps make a large difference.

  • Say "Could you send the €28 for Saturday?" instead of "Some people haven't paid yet."
  • Say "In case it slipped through" instead of "Again."
  • Say "Thanks!" at the end, not "Please advise".
  • Avoid emojis that carry an edge, such as pointed eyes or a clock, when the friendship is still new.
  • Do not post the outstanding amounts publicly, even as a joke. Someone will feel exposed.

Read the message aloud before you send it. If it sounds like something you would say to the friend's face, it is fine. If not, rewrite it plainer.

What if I am the one who forgot to pay someone back?

It happens to everyone. Send the money first, then the message. "So sorry, just sent the €28, thanks for covering it!" resolves the situation in one line and shows that you took it seriously. Do not wait for another reminder, and do not over-apologise; a short, prompt payment is the best apology. If you cannot pay immediately, say when you can, as covered in the section on being the one who owes in how to split and charge friends on Venmo. Being the person who pays promptly is also the best protection against ever needing to send a reminder yourself.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I wait before asking?

A day or two. Sooner is easier for everyone.

What if they still do not pay?

Ask once more, directly and kindly. For small sums, decide if the friendship matters more.

Is it rude to ask for small amounts?

No. Being clear is kinder than quietly resenting it.

Should I remind someone in a group chat?

Post the total and per-person amount to the group, but do not name individuals who have not paid. Message them privately instead.

How do I avoid chasing money in the first place?

Split at the table, use payment requests straight away, or take turns buying rounds so balances even out.

How many reminders are too many?

One message, one follow-up and one direct conversation is plenty. After that, decide whether to forgive it or change how you split with that person.

What should I do if I forgot to pay someone back?

Send the money first, then a short thank-you message. Prompt payment is the best apology.
