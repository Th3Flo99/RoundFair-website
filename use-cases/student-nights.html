Student Nights Out: Fair Rounds on a Budget

RoundFair use case · By Florian Ronsse, founder of FairLead

On a student budget, an extra round you did not drink is not a rounding error. RoundFair keeps the count fair without anyone having to play accountant.

In this guide
  • Big groups, small budgets
  • Settling up without awkward conversations

Running a student night in RoundFair

  1. Start a session and add whoever is out tonight.
  2. Log rounds as they are bought, and who joined each one.
  3. Settle up at the end, or keep the balance and even it out next time.

Big groups, small budgets

Student nights tend to be large and fluid: people join after a lecture, leave for another party, and the group at midnight looks nothing like the group at eight. Strict rotation breaks down quickly in that setting. A record of who paid and who joined each round keeps the balance fair even when the group keeps changing.

If nobody wants to decide who goes next, the app's spin wheel can pick someone at random. There is also a free online version for groups who have not installed the app.

Settling up without awkward conversations

At the end of the night, RoundFair shows everyone's balance and who should pay whom. Sharing a plain-text summary in the group chat means nobody has to chase anyone in person. For tips on asking for money back without souring a friendship, see how to remind friends to pay you back.

Why fairness matters more on a budget

When money is tight, the friend who skips a round or orders the cheapest drink should not end up paying for everyone else. RoundFair records who joined each round, so the balance reflects what people actually had.

Frequently asked questions

Is RoundFair free?

Yes. RoundFair is free on the App Store, with no account needed.

What if someone only joins a few rounds?

Record who joined each round. The balance then reflects what each person took part in, not just how many people were at the table.
