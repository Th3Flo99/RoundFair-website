Coffee and Lunch Rounds at Work: Whose Turn to Pay?

RoundFair use case · By Florian Ronsse, founder of FairLead

Rounds are not only for the pub. Teams that take turns buying coffee or lunch have the same question every day: whose turn is it?

In this guide
  • How a coffee rotation drifts
  • Saving your usual places
  • Do you need an app at all?

Running a coffee rotation in RoundFair

  1. Create a session for the coffee or lunch group and add everyone.
  2. Log each round when someone pays, and who it was for.
  3. Check who is up next before the next coffee run.

How a coffee rotation drifts

A coffee rotation starts simple: one person buys for the group, the next day someone else does. Within a few weeks, holidays, meetings and the colleague who only drinks tea have made the order unclear. Nobody wants to be the one asking whether it is really their turn.

RoundFair treats each coffee run as a round: who paid, and who it was for. The turn order then shows who is furthest behind, so the next person to pay is obvious.

Saving your usual places

Most coffee and lunch rotations use the same few places. Saved spots remember them, including drink prices, so logging the next run takes a few taps. RoundFair can also repeat a previous round without entering everything again.

Do you need an app at all?

For two or three people who see each other every day, memory may be enough. For a bigger team, a shared record avoids the slow build-up of resentment. If your team prefers to keep the rotation in iMessage, a simpler tool may suit you; see RoundFair vs Next One's On Me.

Why a record keeps daily rotations friendly

Small rotations go wrong quietly: holidays, sick days and people who only join on Fridays. A running record shows who is behind without anyone having to keep score in their head.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use RoundFair for coffee instead of drinks?

Yes. A round is any shared purchase one person pays for, so coffee runs and lunch rotations work the same way as rounds at the bar.

What if someone is away for a week?

Only the people who joined a round are part of it, so someone on holiday does not fall behind for coffees they did not have.
